>>> SUMMARY
**The sign-in screen** at `/login`. On a wide screen it is split in two:

- **Left: a living picture of the product** (`Showcase`). The same drifting aurora as the dashboard, a headline ("Run the whole campus *without the paperwork.*"), and four glass "preview cards" (an attendance ring, a paid fee, an approved result, an SGPA) that **float with the mouse** at different depths, like layers of a 3-D scene (`Float` / `usePointer` in `motion/Parallax.tsx`). - **Right: the form.** A username and a password box with icons, an **eye button** to show the password, a **Caps Lock warning**, and a "Sign in" button that shows a spinner while waiting. A small link goes back to the public landing page, and the light/dark switch sits in the corner.

What happens when "Sign in" is pressed:

1. The browser checks that both boxes are filled ("Enter both your username and your password.").
2. `login()` sends `POST /api/auth/login` with `{"username":"student","password":"Edumate@2026"}`.
3. The backend checks the bcrypt hash, the lockout counter and the account, and answers with a **token** and the user's details, or with an error:
   - 401 "Invalid username or password." (the message never says **which** of the two was wrong, so attackers cannot find out which usernames exist);
   - 423 "Too many failed attempts. Your account is locked until 10:45 AM." (15 minutes after the fifth failure)
4. On success, the token is saved and the person is sent to the page they originally asked for, or to the dashboard.

**The demo helper.** In development builds only, a grid of demo accounts appears under the form, with a search box ("admissions", "Student", "exam"…). **Click** a card to fill in its username and the demo password; **double-click** to sign in straight away. A production build (`npm run build`) removes the helper, because `SHOW_DEMO_ACCOUNTS` is false there.

=== FILE frontend/src/pages/LandingPage.tsx
>>> SUMMARY
**The public welcome page** at `/welcome`: EduMate's "shop window", and the page where the **scroll effects** live. A visitor who is not signed in and opens `/` is sent here (`RequireAuth.tsx`); a deep link such as `/results` still goes straight to the login page.

The page, from top to bottom, and the technique behind each effect:

| Section | What the visitor sees | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| Top bar | transparent at first, turns to frosted glass once the page scrolls | `useScrolled(24)` → `data-scrolled="true"` → `backdrop-filter: blur()` in `landing.css` |
| Hero | the headline **slides up word by word**; a product preview below it starts **tilted back in 3-D** and straightens as you scroll; glass cards float with the mouse | `Words` (each word in a clipped box); `useScroll` + `useTransform` → `rotateX`; `Float` / `usePointer` |
| Marquee | two rows of module names gliding in opposite directions, pausing on hover | pure CSS `@keyframes marquee` moving a doubled list by −50 % |
| Numbers | 7 modules, 9 roles, 19 findings, 2 campuses, **counting up** when scrolled into view | `CountUp` inside `Reveal` |
| Modules | a "bento" grid of tiles that appear one after another and light up under the mouse | `Stagger inView` + `followPointer` |
| Journey | the big ring on the left **stays pinned** while the five steps scroll past; the ring fills a fifth per step and the icon and number swap | CSS `position: sticky` + `useInView` with a narrow middle band |
| Roles | scrolling **down** moves a row of role cards **sideways** | a 320vh-tall section with a sticky 100vh window; `useScroll` progress × row width → `x` |
| Quality | the six tested fixes from the SQA report | `Stagger inView` |
| Call to action | an aurora panel with "Sign in" | `Reveal` |

**Smooth scrolling.** All of this sits on top of **Lenis** (`motion/SmoothScroll.tsx`): the wheel does not jump the page in fixed steps but glides it, so scroll-linked effects move fluidly instead of in jerks.

**Reduced motion.** Everything here respects the visitor's "reduce motion" setting: the tilt becomes flat (`useReducedMotion` below), `MotionConfig reducedMotion="user"` in `App.tsx` turns motion's movements into simple fades, `CountUp` shows the final number at once, and `landing.css` stops the marquee.

**Phones.** Below 760 px the sticky effects switch off (`landing.css`): the journey picture is hidden, and the roles become a normal sideways-swipe row with snap points, because pinned sections feel awkward on small touch screens.
